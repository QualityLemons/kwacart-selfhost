Case study · Communication planning

Building shared understanding into every stage of a placement

A workplace team combined five Liberating Structures to shape a strengths-led communication plan: listening before acting, agreeing essential support and learning from experience week by week.

The challenge

Support communication without reducing independence or expectations

A new engineer was preparing for a three-week placement across quality and manufacturing teams. Colleagues needed to support communication through British Sign Language, keyboard responses, written notes and visual prompts while keeping the engineer’s strengths, aspirations and technical capability at the centre of the experience.

Structures
Five connected activities
Timeframe
Three-week placement
Focus
Communication and learning

The approach

Five structures, one communication cycle

Each structure had a distinct job, moving the team from preparation to shared understanding, reflection, discovery and practical adjustment.

Min Specs

Agree the minimum conditions needed before the placement starts.

Conversation Café

Give equal airtime to strengths, concerns, preferences and career goals.

What, So What, Now What?

Review observed interactions and adjust support at the end of each week.

Discovery & Action Dialogue

Find the communication practices already helping successful participation.

Wicked Questions

Hold independence and continuous support—or real expectations and adjustments—at the same time.

Before the placement

Five minimum conditions for success

  • Allow enough time for keyboard responses.
  • Ensure at least one colleague who can use British Sign Language is available.
  • Speak directly to the engineer rather than only through a support person.
  • Provide written and visual instructions wherever possible.
  • Give feedback every day and check understanding rather than assuming it.

The practical output

A three-week communication and review plan

The team translated the activities into named support, regular feedback and review points that could adapt as real experience replaced assumptions.

Assigned support

A primary communication buddy supports introductions and daily check-ins during the quality placement.

Manufacturing support

A second buddy provides shop-floor support and helps with manufacturing terminology.

Daily reflection

A 10-minute end-of-day review captures what worked, what was difficult and what should change tomorrow.

Flexible channels

Communication can use British Sign Language, a talking keyboard, written notes or visual rating scales.

Weekly review

The group considers technical learning, team interaction, confidence, independence and the value the engineer contributed.

Final recommendations

The last review produces placement, employment, workplace-adjustment and personal-development recommendations.

The outcome

A plan designed to learn and improve

Rather than fixing support around assumptions made in advance, the communication plan created a rhythm for observing what happened, recognising strengths and adjusting the next stage.

  1. 01

    Communication preferences and essential support are agreed before work begins.

  2. 02

    The engineer has a direct voice in discussions about strengths, concerns and goals.

  3. 03

    Successful practices are identified from real experience and shared across teams.

  4. 04

    Daily feedback makes small adjustments possible before difficulties accumulate.

  5. 05

    Weekly reviews connect communication support with technical growth and independence.

“Listen first, understand before acting, and create practical support without reducing expectations.”

— Planning principle

Use the pattern

Shape a communication plan around the person and the work

Combine minimum conditions, shared listening and regular reflection so support can evolve with experience.
