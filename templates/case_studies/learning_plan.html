Case study · Learning design

Turning development goals into a year of purposeful practice

A workplace group used What, So What, Now What? to move from observations about an engineering apprentice to a structured learning plan for technical growth, communication, adaptability and resilience.

The opportunity

Design a personalised route to the same professional destination

The apprentice was technically capable, motivated and clear about the skills they wanted to strengthen. The task was not to lower expectations, but to organise real workplace experiences, mentoring and reflection so development would be gradual, supported and visible.

Structure
What, So What, Now What?
Learning goals
Four connected areas
Plan length
12 months

What? · Start with evidence

Separate observations from assumptions

The group first recorded what it knew before interpreting what those facts meant for the learning plan.

Existing capability

The apprentice brought a relevant engineering qualification, technical knowledge and evidence of persistence.

Self-defined goals

Communication, adaptability and resilience were priorities the apprentice had chosen—not deficits imposed by others.

Useful conditions

Clear expectations, preparation time and constructive feedback supported confidence and participation.

Motivation to stretch

The apprentice wanted opportunities to practise and grow rather than having every challenge removed.

Available support

A manager, mentor, colleagues and support worker could form a coordinated learning network.

Real learning contexts

Projects, presentations, team discussions and cross-functional work could make development relevant.

So what? · Find the learning principles

Six principles for effective workplace learning

  • Build the apprenticeship around strengths and professional ambition.
  • Treat communication, adaptability and resilience as formal learning objectives.
  • Embed development in meaningful engineering work rather than separating it into generic training.
  • Use supported challenge instead of avoidance or overprotection.
  • Make inclusion and learning a shared workplace responsibility.
  • Use structure, advance notice, feedback and regular reviews to increase confidence.

Now what? · Sequence the practice

A graduated 12-month learning pathway

Increasingly demanding experiences would develop the target skills through practice, coaching and reflection.

Months 1–3 · Establish foundations

Complete structured tasks, observe workplace routines and take part in small-team interactions with mentor support.

Months 4–6 · Widen participation

Join cross-functional activities, give short presentations and contribute to project discussions.

Months 7–12 · Apply with autonomy

Lead small pieces of work, explain technical solutions and collaborate with less familiar colleagues.

The learning plan

Four goals with actions and evidence of progress

  1. 01

    Technical practice: complete apprenticeship milestones, apply prior knowledge and contribute to engineering projects.

  2. 02

    Communication: ask questions, contribute to meetings and gradually present technical work.

  3. 03

    Adaptability: work across teams, experience planned changes and reflect on strategies that help.

  4. 04

    Resilience: receive feedback, learn from mistakes and recognise successful recovery from setbacks.

“I don’t need to be perfect now. I need opportunities to practise and learn from experience.”

— The apprentice

Making it real

Seven actions for the first 30 days

  • Appoint a workplace mentor.
  • Hold a planning meeting with the apprentice and support worker.
  • Agree measurable learning goals for the next 12 months.
  • Schedule monthly support reviews and quarterly progress reviews.
  • Brief the team on clear and inclusive communication.
  • Create a learning journal and a simple reflection template.
  • Choose the first engineering project that includes communication and teamwork practice.

Use the pattern

Move from experience to insight—and from insight to deliberate practice

Use What, So What, Now What? to distinguish facts from interpretation, identify what matters for learning and agree practical next steps.
