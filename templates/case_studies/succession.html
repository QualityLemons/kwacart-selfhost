Case study · Succession planning

Turning an approaching departure into a shared capability-building plan

A cross-functional group used Discovery & Action Dialogue to expose succession risks, find practices that were already working, and agree immediate actions with named owners.

The situation

The organisation had notice—but preparation had not caught up

A senior leader’s departure was known in advance, yet critical decisions, specialist knowledge and external relationships still depended on one person. Potential successors lacked visibility and practical exposure, while uncertainty was beginning to slow decisions and fuel rumours.

Structure
Discovery & Action Dialogue
Perspectives
Six business functions
Output
A shared 30-day action plan

The dialogue

Move from symptoms to barriers, then learn from people already succeeding

The group worked through seven focused questions: how the problem appears, what effective action looks like, what gets in the way, who already overcomes those barriers, which ideas could spread, what must happen next, and who else needs to be involved.

Warning signs

Decision authority was unclear, rumours replaced communication, and important work still waited for one individual.

Hidden dependency

Tacit knowledge, stakeholder trust and escalation judgement were concentrated rather than shared.

Everyday barriers

Today’s delivery pressures made coaching and delegation feel slower than doing the work directly.

Positive deviants

Some managers already delegated real responsibility, cross-trained teams and involved emerging leaders early.

Future needs

The group challenged the assumption that succession meant reproducing the existing role without redesign.

Shared ownership

Leadership, HR, process owners, potential successors and key external stakeholders all had a part to play.

What the group surfaced

The gap was not talent. It was deliberate transfer.

  • Knowledge and decision-making logic remained concentrated.
  • Customer, supplier and regulatory relationships needed wider ownership.
  • Development was happening informally rather than through a visible plan.
  • Potential successors needed real experience, not information alone.
  • Succession had to become business continuity work, not an HR-only exercise.

The outcome

Six actions for the next 30 days

  1. 01

    Sponsor a review of the role’s responsibilities, decisions, relationships, risks and specialist knowledge.

  2. 02

    Identify potential successors and give them exposure to meetings, reviews and real decisions.

  3. 03

    Capture tacit knowledge, including judgement, escalation triggers, unwritten rules and lessons learned.

  4. 04

    Map single points of failure and spread capability through shadowing and cross-training.

  5. 05

    Broaden ownership of key customer, supplier, auditor and partner relationships.

  6. 06

    Review the role against future business needs rather than simply copying its current shape.

“The best succession plans are not replacement plans. They spread knowledge, authority, relationships and leadership capacity.”

— Group’s final recap
