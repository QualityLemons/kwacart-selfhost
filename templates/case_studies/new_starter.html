Case study · New-starter onboarding

Turning the team’s experience into a new manager’s first 90-day plan

A new quality manager used Wise Crowds to hear practical advice from colleagues across the business—before deciding where to focus, what to learn, and how to build credibility.

The situation

A cross-functional role with no formal authority

The new starter needed to influence operations, production, maintenance, engineering and supply chain. They wanted to understand the business quickly, make quality feel like a shared responsibility, and avoid the common mistake of arriving with solutions before learning how work really happens.

Structure
Wise Crowds
Perspectives
Six business functions
Output
A focused 90-day action plan

The approach

Ask the people who understand the system from different angles

Colleagues took the role of consultants. They first asked clarifying questions, then offered advice from the realities of their own functions.

Operations

Follow work from quotation to delivery and connect quality to cost, delivery, safety and customer outcomes.

Production

Spend time on the shop floor, listen to operators and build trust with supervisors before proposing controls.

Quality engineering

Map processes, owners, metrics, risks and recurring issues to see the organisation as one connected system.

Maintenance

Look beyond operator error to equipment condition, calibration, settings, tooling and maintenance practices.

Supply chain

Include supplier approval, monitoring and corrective action in the overall picture of quality performance.

Manufacturing engineering

Use data to learn—not simply to measure—and choose one visible cross-functional problem to improve together.

What the group surfaced

Credibility comes from enabling the business, not policing it

  • Build relationships before making major changes.
  • Understand real work in practice, not only procedures on paper.
  • Connect quality to outcomes each department already values.
  • Use data to reveal systemic patterns rather than assign blame.
  • Deliver one meaningful cross-functional improvement early.

The outcome

A practical first-90-days agenda

  1. 01

    Spend significant time observing processes where the work happens.

  2. 02

    Build relationships and credibility before attempting major changes.

  3. 03

    Map the enterprise as an interconnected quality system.

  4. 04

    Learn the business metrics that matter to each function.

  5. 05

    Deliver an early improvement that solves a genuine business problem.

“Enterprise quality is really about connecting people, processes, data and objectives across the organisation.”

— Session participant’s closing reflection
