Case study · Person-centred planning

Designing an apprenticeship around strengths, growth and belonging

An engineering apprentice, their support worker and workplace colleagues used Conversation Café to create a plan that combined technical ambition with clear support for communication, adaptability and resilience.

The starting point

Ask what success should feel like to the person—not only what support they need

The apprentice arrived with an engineering qualification, clear technical strengths and a desire to become more confident working with people and handling change. The challenge was to provide meaningful opportunities to grow without compromising wellbeing, confidence or inclusion.

Structure
Conversation Café
Voices
Apprentice and support network
Horizon
The first apprenticeship year

What shifted through listening

The conversation moved from accommodation to supported growth

After everyone spoke and listened without interruption, the group’s framing changed in five important ways.

Strengths before barriers

Start with technical capability, persistence and professional ambition rather than defining the person by difficulty.

Growth, not protection

Provide manageable challenges with preparation and reflection instead of removing every unfamiliar situation.

Explicit expectations

Make schedules, feedback and workplace norms clear rather than relying on assumptions or unwritten rules.

Practice through real work

Develop communication and adaptability inside meaningful engineering projects, not only through classroom training.

A workplace that adapts too

Help colleagues communicate clearly, allow processing time and avoid interpreting a pause as disengagement.

Shared responsibility

Coordinate the apprentice, manager, mentor and support worker around one plan and a consistent set of goals.

The shared principles

Inclusion is a shared responsibility

  • The apprenticeship should build on capability, not attempt to “fix” the person.
  • Communication, adaptability and resilience deserve goals, feedback and recognition alongside technical competence.
  • Challenges should increase gradually, with coaching and reflection afterwards.
  • Clear explanations—including why something changed—can reduce uncertainty.
  • No single manager, mentor or support worker should carry responsibility for success alone.

Development in real work

A three-stage communication pathway

The group designed progression that made expectations predictable while increasing complexity over time.

Months 1–3 · Build safety

Use daily mentor check-ins, small-team discussions, structured introductions and supported practice asking questions.

Months 4–6 · Increase participation

Present progress on a small task, contribute to team meetings and take part in toolbox talks with advance notice.

Months 7–12 · Share expertise

Explain technical work, support external visits and lead part of a project review with coaching available.

The outcome

Four goals shaped around the apprentice

The conversation produced a draft person-centred plan in which personal aspirations, workplace responsibilities and practical support reinforced one another.

  1. 01

    Become a capable electrical engineering apprentice by completing milestones and applying existing knowledge.

  2. 02

    Build communication confidence through progressively greater participation and technical explanation.

  3. 03

    Develop adaptability through controlled changes, clear reasons and reflection on useful strategies.

  4. 04

    Strengthen resilience by learning from setbacks, seeking help and recognising successful recovery.

“I want to contribute and be part of the team.”

— The apprentice

The support commitments

Turn the plan into a coordinated working agreement

  • Assign a trusted workplace mentor and hold regular one-to-one reviews.
  • Agree first-year goals with the apprentice and support worker.
  • Brief colleagues on clear, patient and respectful communication.
  • Create graduated opportunities to participate in meetings, presentations and projects.
  • Review the plan regularly and celebrate personal growth as actively as technical achievement.

Use the pattern

Build plans with people, not for them

Use Conversation Café to hear the person’s aspirations alongside the perspectives of those who can help turn them into consistent, practical support.
