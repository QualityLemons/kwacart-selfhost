Case study · Grant development

Turning six bold ideas into one compelling environmental grant concept

A cross-functional group used 25/10 Crowd Sourcing to generate, score and combine ideas for a primary school project with measurable impact, strong pupil ownership and a lasting community legacy.

The opportunity

Find the idea with enough ambition—and enough evidence—to interest funders

The group wanted an environmental project that would do more than raise awareness. It needed to involve pupils in real problem-solving, create visible improvements at school, connect education with local industry, and produce evidence that could demonstrate value over time.

Structure
25/10 Crowd Sourcing
Ideas tested
Six bold proposals
Output
One integrated grant concept

The crowd-sourcing round

Each idea paired a bold ambition with a practical first step

Participants proposed ideas independently, then scored them over five rounds. The ranking showed which qualities the group considered both bold and actionable.

24/25 · Living laboratory

Pupils measure energy, waste, water and biodiversity, producing real evidence of environmental improvement.

23/25 · Eco-innovators

Children identify problems, design solutions and see the strongest ideas implemented for real.

21/25 · Environmental engineers

Employees mentor pupils as they design practical sustainability improvements for their school.

20/25 · Renewable energy zone

Solar technology, live energy displays and demonstrations create a visible, hands-on learning asset.

18/25 · Biodiversity corridor

Pupil-led habitats and pollinator planting connect science learning with a tangible natural legacy.

17/25 · Circular economy

Local businesses provide surplus materials for pupils to transform into useful environmental assets.

What the scores revealed

The strongest proposition was bigger than any single idea

  • Pupils should be researchers, designers and problem-solvers—not passive recipients.
  • Environmental improvements should be measured so impact is visible to funders.
  • Hands-on STEM learning should lead to change in the real world.
  • School, business and community partnerships should extend the benefits.
  • The project should leave a lasting physical and educational legacy.

The outcome

A Primary School Environmental Innovation Hub

Rather than choose one activity, the group combined the highest-value elements into a single coherent grant concept with five reinforcing strands.

  1. 01

    A living laboratory measuring energy, waste, water and biodiversity.

  2. 02

    Eco-innovation challenges shaped and led by pupils.

  3. 03

    Biodiversity and renewable-energy improvements pupils can see and study.

  4. 04

    Mentoring and material support from local businesses.

  5. 05

    Evidence collected over time to show environmental, educational and community impact.

“The highest-scoring ideas combined education with measurable environmental outcomes. They weren’t just awareness projects.”

— Group debrief
