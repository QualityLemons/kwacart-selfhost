Case study · Positive behaviour

Rebuilding trust through small, consistent workplace behaviours

A workplace group used Min Specs and Project Vision Map to replace blame with a practical, non-punitive approach to communication, accountability and inclusive working relationships.

The challenge

Strengthen relationships without making one person the problem

An apprentice, managers and colleagues were experiencing growing tension. Earlier support commitments had drifted, misunderstandings were not always addressed directly, and outside support was increasingly needed to repair relationships. The group wanted better everyday habits—not blame, special treatment or reduced performance expectations.

Structures
Two connected sessions
Timeframe
Six months
Focus
Behaviour and relationships

Part one · Min Specs

Sift a long wish list down to what must happen

The group tested each proposed rule by asking whether its absence would place the apprenticeship and working relationships at serious risk. Desirable but unsustainable requirements were dropped or made more proportionate.

Practical notice

Give advance notice where reasonably possible, while still developing strategies for unexpected change.

Proportionate support

Match support to the challenge rather than removing every difficulty or creating dependency.

Purposeful involvement

Bring in support professionals for significant issues and reviews—not every routine conversation.

Early repair

Accept that misunderstandings happen, then address them quickly before they become relationship problems.

What survived the test

Eight essential everyday behaviours

  • Communicate clearly and directly.
  • Hold regular one-to-one check-ins.
  • Discuss concerns with the person, not about them.
  • Honour the support commitments already agreed.
  • Continue offering meaningful opportunities for growth and challenge.
  • Share responsibility for inclusion and success.
  • Maintain active mentoring and coaching.
  • Address misunderstandings and tensions early.

Part two · Project Vision Map

A six-month reflection and inclusion project

With the minimum conditions clear, the group designed a project to make those behaviours part of normal working life.

Leadership commitment

Managers recommit visibly, protect time for one-to-ones and participate in reflection.

Safe reflection

Facilitated, non-punitive sessions let people discuss frustrations, mistakes and improvements honestly.

Full participation

The apprentice helps shape decisions, explains what works and develops greater self-advocacy.

Structured mentoring

Scheduled check-ins and reflection after difficult situations turn support into a daily practice.

Shared expectations

Everyone understands the support commitments, responsibilities and development goals.

Regular review

Monthly reviews check whether agreed support and the Min Specs are actually happening.

The intended difference

A team that handles challenges well

Success was not defined as the absence of difficulty. It meant stronger habits for raising concerns, giving feedback, keeping commitments and repairing misunderstandings before trust eroded.

  1. 01

    Regular mentoring and one-to-one conversations take place consistently.

  2. 02

    Concerns are discussed directly, using observable behaviour rather than assumptions.

  3. 03

    The apprentice feels supported, appropriately challenged and increasingly independent.

  4. 04

    Managers become more confident in honest performance and development conversations.

  5. 05

    Outside support becomes strategic rather than a recurring crisis response.

  6. 06

    The organisation gains a repeatable approach for people with different communication styles.

“Keep commitments. Communicate clearly. Stay connected. Solve problems early. Grow together.”

— Group conclusion

Use the pattern

Design the conditions that make positive behaviour easier to sustain

Use Min Specs to identify the few behaviours that cannot be lost, then Project Vision Map to build the commitment, practice and review needed to sustain them.
